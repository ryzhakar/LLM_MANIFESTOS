## Method

The review read lines 1 to 139 of the target in full, and every tool returned its whole output. It ran the five checks and the two closing checks on every sentence and tested every rule against the manifesto's own text, since the manifesto addresses "the writer of any text that asks a reader to meet a standard" and is such a text itself. The review left grammar alone, as the dispatch asked. It tested the claims about people and models for agreement with the rest of the page, not against the literature.

Each row of the violation table names one offending text and every rule it fails, and the count is the number of rows. The rows run most serious first, and the index after the table groups them by rule number. Each rewrite is the smallest text that meets the row's rules. Where an instruction lacks its reason, the rewrite adds a reason drawn from the page itself or a purpose clause, so that no new empirical claim enters the text.

## Judgment

Read by a writer or a model, the manifesto does not raise its voice, and it raises the bar only in part. No sentence threatens, flatters, or aims at the writer, and its pressure comes from evidence about readers. It misses its own bar where a reader will copy it most. The belief wording at line 48, which line 84 holds up as the model, rests on no ground, though line 49 requires one. The standard at line 19 arrives without the reason that the first principle requires beside it. Line 96 says that no study tests a rule here on a model, while lines 66 and 99 report findings on models. The worked example claims to pass every check, yet it states one non-negotiable twice and names its failure twice. Most of the other rows are instructions without the reason that line 98 requires for a model reader, and action steps that name the thing to avoid. The last line, "Raise the bar, not the voice", names the very failure that line 60 says draws errors toward it.
